# Customer Segmentation with RFM Analysis & K-Means Clustering

An end-to-end unsupervised machine learning project that validates clustering tendency and segments e-commerce customers into actionable tiers using the **UCI Online Retail** dataset.

---

## Project Overview

Rather than applying clustering blindly, this workflow first verifies whether the customer data contains meaningful structure using the **Hopkins Statistic**, determines the optimal number of segments via the **Elbow Method** and **Silhouette Score**, and profiles the resulting customer groups using **RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary)** metrics.

### Key Results
* **Hopkins Statistic (`pyclustertend`):** `0.0570` *(on `pyclustertend`'s inverted scale where `< 0.25` indicates strong clusterability, equivalent to `0.9430` on the standard scale)*
* **Optimal Clusters (`k`):** `3` distinct customer segments
* **Dataset Scope:** ~4,338 unique customers aggregated from 500,000+ raw retail transactions

---

## Methodology & Pipeline

### 1. Data Cleaning
Raw transaction logs from the [UCI Online Retail Dataset (ID: 352)](https://uci.edu) were cleaned by:
* Removing records with missing `CustomerID` values.
* Filtering out cancelled transactions (`InvoiceNo` starting with `'C'`).
* Removing rows with zero or negative `Quantity` and `UnitPrice` values.

### 2. RFM Feature Engineering & Preprocessing
Transactions were aggregated to the customer level across three behavioral dimensions:
* **Recency:** Days since the customer's most recent purchase.
* **Frequency:** Total number of unique invoices/orders placed.
* **Monetary:** Total revenue generated by the customer.

Because retail spend and order frequency are heavily right-skewed due to B2B wholesale accounts, features were log-transformed (`np.log1p`) and standardized (`StandardScaler`) so no single feature dominated distance calculations.

### 3. Cluster Tendency & Model Selection
* **Hopkins Test:** Computed on a 10% sample of the scaled RFM matrix (`0.0570`), confirming strong non-random clustering structure.
* **K-Means Evaluation:** Tested `k = 2` through `k = 10` using **Inertia** (within-cluster sum of squares) and **Silhouette Score**, identifying **`k = 3`** as the optimal balance between cluster compactness, separation, and business interpretability.

---

## Customer Segments (`k = 3`)

| Segment | Behavioral Profile | Business Takeaway |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Champions & Wholesale VIPs** | Lowest recency, highest purchase frequency, and highest total spend. | Smallest group by headcount, but drives the majority of revenue. Ideal for VIP retention perks and dedicated account support. |
| **Loyal Regulars** | Moderate recency with steady repeat orders and mid-tier spend. | Core dependable customer base. Prime candidates for cross-selling, bundles, and loyalty tier upgrades. |
| **At-Risk & One-Time Shoppers** | Highest recency (haven't purchased in months), low order count, and low spend. | Largest customer group, largely seasonal or one-time buyers. Best suited for automated win-back emails rather than high ad spend. |

## Visualizations

* **Elbow & Silhouette Curves:** Evaluates inertia and cluster separation across `k = 2` to `10` to justify `k = 3`.
* **2D PCA Scatter Plot:** Projects the 3D standardized RFM space onto two principal components to visually confirm cluster separation.
* **RFM Snake Plot:** Plots each segment's standardized mean (z-score) across Recency, Frequency, and Monetary relative to the overall customer average.
